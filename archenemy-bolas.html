Archenemy: Nicol Bolas, play guide

For our 20 oversized scheme cards (Archenemy: Nicol Bolas, 2017). Card text is the same as in Commander Life's scheme deck. Schemes are an Archenemy-only card type, so Commander legality doesn't apply to them.

The bottom line

Archenemy: one player takes 20 schemes and 40 life against everyone else (20 life each). Every turn you flip the top scheme and it fires. You win by knocking out all the heroes; they win together by knocking you out.

Heroes: you can't remove a scheme, so beat the person. Save removal and burst for the archenemy, and plan around the three scheme clocks (below) instead of fighting each one.

Setup

  • The archenemy shuffles the 20 schemes face down in the command zone. Nobody looks at them.
  • Archenemy starts at 40 life, heroes at 20 each (our Commander Life app does this itself under Players → AE).
  • The archenemy goes first and skips their first draw like anyone going first.
  • Commander damage (our house version): heroes take it only from the archenemy's commander; the archenemy takes it from each hero's. 21 from one commander is lethal.
  • Heroes are one team: they win and lose together and don't attack each other.

The archenemy's turn

  1. Untap, upkeep, draw as normal.
  2. Precombat main phase, first thing: turn the top scheme face up. That is "setting it in motion". This happens before you play a land or cast anything.
  3. Its "when you set this scheme in motion" text resolves. An ongoing scheme stays face up and keeps working. Any other scheme goes to the bottom of the deck when it's done.
  4. Then the turn goes on: land, spells, combat. You set one scheme per turn, even with ongoing ones already out.
  5. Ongoing schemes end when they say so (see the heroes' outs) or when you abandon them. An abandoned scheme goes to the bottom.

The deck runs through all 20 before any scheme comes round again, so on turn 21 you meet the first one again unless an ongoing scheme held up the cycle. Heroes can keep track of what's already been seen.

Rules that trip people up

  • Schemes aren't permanents or spells. You can't counter, destroy or exile one. You can only deal with the effect that already happened (a token, a stolen creature).
  • The archenemy makes every choice in "choose an opponent" or "choose a target". Where it says each opponent, each hero decides their own part (which creature to keep, which card to discard, which pile).
  • "Six or more lands" is checked as the scheme resolves, after any land you played earlier. This scheme turns on at land six: Power Without Equal, Pay Tribute to Me, Behold My Grandeur.
  • Free spells (When Will You Learn?, Power Without Equal) are cast while the scheme resolves. Pay no mana cost but follow the rest of the rules (targets, additional costs). Lands can't be cast, so an exiled land stays exiled.
  • Horror tokens are 3/3 black Horror creatures. They're ordinary creatures, so they're summoning sick unless a card says otherwise.
  • If the deck runs dry, shuffle all abandoned schemes and start again (not an issue with 20 cards unless several ongoing schemes are stuck face up).
  • Tie on highest life (The Mighty Will Fall): the archenemy picks.

Three clocks for the heroes

Every ongoing scheme ends at an opponent's end step if that hero pays a price. All the checks run on that hero's own end step, so each hero decides for themselves.

Ongoing schemeThe price to end it
Bow to My CommandTap untapped creatures with total power 8 or more
Because I Have Willed ItCast 4 or more spells in one turn
My Forces Are InnumerableSacrifice two creatures
My Laughter EchoesNo price: only the archenemy can abandon it

Heroes can plan these ahead. If Bow to My Command is out, leave 8 power untapped at the end of your turn. If Innumerable is out, keep two cheap creatures around to feed it (a token or a chump).

All 20 schemes

Plan by turn count

  • Turns 1 to 5, no six lands. Aim to set up. Behold My Grandeur and Pay Tribute to Me are weaker here, so the early threats are single removal (Make Yourself Useful, There Is No Refuge) and the tokens.
  • Land six and up. Three schemes turn on. Heroes who can hit the archenemy's lands should.
  • Life. 40 sounds like a lot but The Mighty Will Fall plus a 3/3 token every turn adds up. Heroes who hold their burst for one turn win; heroes who poke early just get sacrificed for.

For the archenemy: how to pick a line

  • Save the free spells. Power Without Equal and When Will You Learn? are worth the most when your hand and the exiled cards are full of expensive spells.
  • Edicts want an empty board. No One Will Hear Your Cries, Pay Tribute to Me and The Mighty Will Fall hit hardest on heroes with one big creature or one big life total.
  • Keep a creature for Bow. You need the chosen hero's creatures to be a real threat; pick the hero with the biggest board.
  • Know when your ongoing schemes will fall. If the heroes can't pay, they stay forever. Count their power, spells cast and creatures before each end step.
  • My Laughter Echoes is a 2-for-1 on your next non-ongoing scheme, so keep it up as long as you can and never abandon it early.

Our two modes in Commander Life

  • App's deck: Menu → Players → AE, then Schemes → App's deck. A scheme button appears on the archenemy's seat. Open the deck builder, tap The cards we own to load all 20, then Deal. Set in motion, Done, Keep it in play, Abandon, Undo and History are all there.
  • Our cards: Schemes → Our cards. The app only keeps life and commander damage; you flip the real cards.
  • New game shuffles all 20 back in. Changing the archenemy starts the game over.
